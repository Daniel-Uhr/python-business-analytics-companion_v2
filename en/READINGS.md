# Open readings by chapter

These readings complement the book’s classic references.

## 01

-   *Introduction to Modern Statistics* (Çetinkaya-Rundel and Hardin 2024), Chapter 2, "Study design". Compare sample selection and random assignment; identify how coverage and participation limit generalization. [Free online reading](https://openintro-ims.netlify.app/).

## 02

-   *Introductory Business Statistics* (Holmes, Illowsky, and Dean 2023), Chapter 3, "Probability Topics". Review events, probability rules, and conditioning. State the information used in the denominator of each calculation. [Free online reading](https://openstax.org/books/introductory-business-statistics-2e/pages/preface).

## 03

-   *Think Stats: Exploratory Data Analysis in Python* (Downey 2025), Chapter 8, "Estimation". Connect repeated sampling, standard error, and confidence intervals. Compare the variability of the statistic with the spread of individual observations. [Free online reading](https://allendowney.github.io/ThinkStats/).

## 04

-   *Introduction to Modern Statistics* (Çetinkaya-Rundel and Hardin 2024), Chapters 13--14, "Inference with mathematical models" and "Decision errors". Review the conditions of the procedures and decision errors. Explain separately what the interval and the test say about the business contrast. [Free online reading](https://openintro-ims.netlify.app/).

## 05

-   *Think Stats: Exploratory Data Analysis in Python* (Downey 2025), Chapter 7, "Relationships between variables". Compare scatterplots and measures of association. Discuss what a correlation can conceal about shape, extreme observations, and sample composition. [Free online reading](https://allendowney.github.io/ThinkStats/).

## 06

-   *An Introduction to Statistical Learning: with Applications in Python* (James et al. 2023), Chapter 3, "Linear Regression", simple-regression discussion and lab. Compare estimation, slope interpretation, and residual analysis. Contrast the units of the example with those of this chapter's application. [Free online reading](https://www.statlearning.com/).

## 07

-   *An Introduction to Statistical Learning: with Applications in Python* (James et al. 2023), Chapter 3, "Multiple Linear Regression". Read coefficients in relation to the variables held constant. Check how the model's composition changes the comparison being made. [Free online reading](https://www.statlearning.com/).

-   *Think Stats: Exploratory Data Analysis in Python* (Downey 2025), Chapter 11, "Multiple Regression". Follow an analysis in Python and explain why controlling for variables is not, by itself, sufficient to establish causality. [Free online reading](https://allendowney.github.io/ThinkStats/).

## 08

-   *Introduction to Modern Statistics* (Çetinkaya-Rundel and Hardin 2024), Chapter 25, "Inference for linear regression with multiple predictors". Relate coefficient uncertainty to the hypotheses being assessed. Keep statistical significance separate from business relevance. [Free online reading](https://openintro-ims.netlify.app/).

## 09

-   *Introduction to Econometrics with R* (Hanck et al. 2026), Section 8.3, "Interactions between Independent Variables". Compare interactions between indicators, between an indicator and a continuous variable, and between two continuous variables. This is conceptual reading; the examples use R. [Free online reading](https://www.econometrics-with-r.org/). Online version dated April 4, 2026; the year identifies the consulted update.

-   *An Introduction to Statistical Learning: with Applications in Python* (James et al. 2023), Chapter 3 and lab, qualitative predictors and interactions. Reconstruct the reference category and conditional slope before interpreting the Python output. [Free online reading](https://www.statlearning.com/).

## 10

-   *Introduction to Econometrics with R* (Hanck et al. 2026), Chapter 8, "Nonlinear Regression Functions". Focus on logarithms and polynomials; relate each coefficient to the units of the dependent variable. The examples use R. [Free online reading](https://www.econometrics-with-r.org/). Online version dated April 4, 2026; the year identifies the consulted update.

-   *An Introduction to Statistical Learning: with Applications in Python* (James et al. 2023), Chapter 3 and lab, nonlinear transformations of predictors. Compare the construction of polynomial terms in Python with the local interpretation of the slope. [Free online reading](https://www.statlearning.com/).

## 11

-   *Introduction to Econometrics with R* (Hanck et al. 2026), Section 5.4, "Heteroskedasticity and Homoskedasticity". Compare conditional dispersion and conventional versus robust standard errors. The examples use R; the reading complements the Python implementation. [Free online reading](https://www.econometrics-with-r.org/). Online version dated April 4, 2026; the year identifies the consulted update.

## 12

-   *An Introduction to Statistical Learning: with Applications in Python* (James et al. 2023), Chapter 3 and lab, regression diagnostics. Examine residuals, leverage, and influential observations; explain what each diagnostic can reveal about the business application. [Free online reading](https://www.statlearning.com/).

-   *Introduction to Econometrics with R* (Hanck et al. 2026), Section 9.1, "Internal and External Validity". Use the distinction between internal and external validity to organize the limitations of the assessment. Do not infer causality merely from a good fit. [Free online reading](https://www.econometrics-with-r.org/). Online version dated April 4, 2026; the year identifies the consulted update.

## 13

-   *An Introduction to Statistical Learning: with Applications in Python* (James et al. 2023), Chapter 4, logistic regression and lab. Relate coefficients, predicted probabilities, and model evaluation; distinguish probabilistic prediction from threshold-based classification. [Free online reading](https://www.statlearning.com/).

-   *Introduction to Modern Statistics* (Çetinkaya-Rundel and Hardin 2024), Chapters 9 and 26, logistic regression and its inference. Review the interpretation of coefficients and uncertainty, returning to the contrast relevant to the decision. [Free online reading](https://openintro-ims.netlify.app/).

## 14

-   *An Introduction to Statistical Learning: with Applications in Python* (James et al. 2023), Section 4.6, "Generalized Linear Models", and Poisson lab. Follow the bicycle-rental example and interpret the predicted mean. This reading does not replace the deeper treatment of NB2 and exposure. [Free online reading](https://www.statlearning.com/).

## 15

-   *Forecasting: Principles and Practice, the Pythonic Way* (Hyndman et al. 2026), Chapters 2 and 5 and Section 9.3, "Autoregressive models". Connect time-series graphics, forecast evaluation, and autoregression. Prioritize the horizon and temporal separation between training and evaluation. [Free online reading](https://otexts.com/fpppy/).

-   *Think Stats: Exploratory Data Analysis in Python* (Downey 2025), Chapter 12, "Time Series Analysis". Compare exploration of a series in Python with identification of its patterns and forecasting limits. [Free online reading](https://allendowney.github.io/ThinkStats/).

## 16

-   *Forecasting: Principles and Practice, the Pythonic Way* (Hyndman et al. 2026), Chapter 3 and Section 9.1, "Stationarity and differencing". Compare trend, decomposition, and differencing. Relate unit-root tests to the specification and economic interpretation of the series. [Free online reading](https://otexts.com/fpppy/).

## 17

-   *Forecasting: Principles and Practice, the Pythonic Way* (Hyndman et al. 2026), Chapter 1, "Getting started". Use the discussion of the forecasting process to review problem definition, available information, and evaluation when the project includes a temporal question. [Free online reading](https://otexts.com/fpppy/).

-   *Introduction to Econometrics with R* (Hanck et al. 2026), Section 9.1, "Internal and External Validity". Review the population reached and the limitations of the conclusions. Distinguish the validity of the argument from the numerical quality of the fit. [Free online reading](https://www.econometrics-with-r.org/). Online version dated April 4, 2026; the year identifies the consulted update.
