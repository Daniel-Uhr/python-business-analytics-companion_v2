## Solution to Exercise 01 — Commuting Survey

- **Target population:** students regularly enrolled in the semester.
- **Unit:** student.
- **Sampling frame:** the registry of eligible enrollments.
- **Observed sample:** 252 students with valid responses, not the 300 initially selected.
- **Parameter of interest:** the average commuting time of all students in the target population.

Distinguishing between those selected and those who responded makes the nonresponse stage visible.

## Solution to Exercise 02 — Rule and Result

The parameter of interest is the population mean commuting time. The estimator is the rule “average of the times in the sample.” The estimate is 38.6 minutes, the value obtained from the 252 responses.

The estimate is computed from the sample; the parameter refers to the entire target population and remains unknown. How close the two are depends on the process that produced the data.

```python
example_times = [32.0, 41.0, 38.0, 45.0, 37.0]
example_estimate = sum(example_times) / len(example_times)
print(f"Example of the sample-mean rule: {example_estimate:.1f} minutes")
```

```text
Example of the sample-mean rule: 38.6 minutes
```

The five times in the code are illustrative values chosen only to reproduce the 38.6-minute mean; they are not the survey's 252 responses. The code shows that the estimator is a rule that can be applied to different samples. The numerical result produced in each set is an estimate; on its own, it does not reveal the population mean.

## Solution to Exercise 03 — Unit and Granularity

The question requires one unit per **customer**, because it asks for a proportion of customers. Order rows would treat customers with many purchases as if they were several units, giving them greater weight. Before estimating the proportion, we would need to build customer-level information: whether or not the customer made a repeat purchase.

## Solution to Exercise 04 — Census or Sample?

The extract is a census of the records in the system, but not necessarily a census of the target population if eligible contract workers are missing. The problem is one of coverage: observing every row of an incomplete frame is not the same as observing every unit of the intended population.

## Solution to Exercise 05 — Formulating the Parameter

| Question | Target population | Unit | Parameter of interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Digital ticket | eligible digital purchases in the second quarter | transaction | average value of the eligible purchases |
| Default | contracts in the defined cohort, followed for 90 days | contract | proportion of contracts in default after 90 days |
| Industrial satisfaction | eligible employees at the industrial plants on the defined date | employee | average satisfaction of the defined population |

Period, eligibility, and unit make the parameter operationally clear.

## Summary

The target population, unit, and parameter are defined by the question. The sampling frame and the sample describe how we reached the data. The estimator is the rule; the estimate is the observed result.

## Solution to Exercise 06 — The Path to the Sample

```text
4,800 workers in the target population
→ people registered in and reachable through the app
→ people who received/viewed the invitation
→ voluntary participants
→ 1,900 valid responses
```

Contract workers without access can drop out at the sampling frame; infrequent users may not see the invitation; people who see it may not respond; incomplete responses may still leave the final dataset.

## Solution to Exercise 07 — Coverage

If contract workers belong to the target population, their absence from the app creates undercoverage. If the question were explicitly restricted to employees with access to the app, the target population would be different. Changing the population can change the assessment, but it should not be done after the fact just to legitimize the available dataset.

## Solution to Exercise 08 — Nonresponse and Convenience

Nonresponse occurs among those who received the invitation and did not provide a valid response. Voluntary participation may also select people who are more engaged or who have had more intense experiences. We do not know the direction without additional evidence; the rigorous point is that the propensity to respond may be related to the perception of safety.

## Solution to Exercise 09 — Does Size Solve It?

No. The share of almost 40% describes quantity; it does not correct undercoverage or self-selection. Many responses can reduce internal fluctuations among participants, but they still inadequately represent workers who are absent from the app or who have a different propensity to respond.

```python
workers = 4_800
responses = 1_900
observed_percentage = responses / workers * 100
print(f"Responses relative to the stated total: {observed_percentage:.1f}%")
```

```text
Responses relative to the stated total: 39.6%
```

The calculation gives approximately 39.6%, but it does not measure representativeness. It must be read together with the coverage and response design.

## Solution to Exercise 10 — Limits and Improvements

Responsible conclusion: the dataset describes the 1,900 respondents and may signal relevant experiences, but it does not automatically support an estimate for all 4,800 workers, because coverage and participation differ across groups.

Two improvements act at different stages: offering in-person channels or accessible terminals reduces undercoverage; sending reminders and planned follow-up to those selected reduces nonresponse. A design stratified by area and type of employment can also guarantee selection opportunities to the substantive groups.

## Summary

Representativeness must be defended through the correspondence among the target population, frame, selection, and response. Sample size alone does not replace this audit.

## Solution to Exercise 11 — Objects of Inference

- **Unit:** a small business in operation.
- **Parameter of interest:** the proportion of small businesses in the target population that plan to hire in the next six months.
- **Estimator:** the proportion of affirmative responses in the observed sample.

The rule can be the same in both designs, but its ability to inform the parameter depends on how the sample was formed.

## Solution to Exercise 12 — Comparing the Mechanisms

Design Alpha uses an open link, has no known probabilistic selection, and mainly reaches digitally active members. There is self-selection and limited coverage of the other businesses.

Design Beta starts from an up-to-date city business registry, draws units at random, and follows up nonrespondents. Its mechanism is more transparent, but the frame covers only registered businesses, and there are still 90 nonrespondents. Therefore, it does not automatically represent unregistered businesses either.

```python
beta_selected = 600
beta_responses = 510
beta_response_rate = beta_responses / beta_selected * 100
print(f"Response rate in Design Beta: {beta_response_rate:.1f}%")
```

```text
Response rate in Design Beta: 85.0%
```

The 85.0% response rate is informative about the stage after the draw, but it does not solve the absence of unregistered businesses from the frame.

## Solution to Exercise 13 — Argued Decision

Beta offers a more defensible basis because it has a defined frame, a random draw, and follow-up. The best-supported conclusion concerns **registered small businesses covered by the registry**, not all businesses in operation.

Its main limitation is the undercoverage of unregistered businesses, in addition to the possible difference of the 90 nonrespondents. Alpha does not become superior because of its 2,400 responses: the larger volume preserves the open-link mechanism and the concentration on digitally active members.

## Solution to Exercise 14 — Changing the Target Population

If the target population were genuinely defined before data collection as digitally active member businesses, Alpha's coverage would be more coherent. Even so, the open link would maintain self-selection among those who saw it and decided to respond. The assessment improves because frame and population move closer together, but it does not automatically become perfect.

## Solution to Exercise 15 — Executive Communication

We recommend Design Beta to estimate the proportion of businesses that intend to hire among registered small businesses covered by the city registry. The sample proportion is the estimator, but the presence of nonrespondents and the absence of unregistered businesses limit generalization. The 2,400 open-link responses do not outweigh this evidence on size alone, because they reflect self-selection and outreach concentrated on digitally active member businesses.

## Summary

The preferred design is not chosen on the basis of `n` alone. The decision combines the target population, sampling frame, selection mechanism, response, and explicit limits.

### Transfer within Exercise 15 — Checking before publication

In another entirely hypothetical scenario, 600 eligible, unique enterprises were randomly selected. The file contains 500 forms with 180 positive responses. Verification identifies 20 excess duplicate forms, 12 of them positive. The validation rule confirms which record to retain per enterprise; the remaining 480 records have definite item responses.

Calculate the proportion of positive forms before verification, the proportion of positive enterprises afterward, and the correct response rate. Calculate the logical extremes for intentions among the 600 selected enterprises. Write a public statement that does not confuse those extremes with a confidence interval or intentions with completed hires. Explain what is still missing to discuss the city's informal businesses.

```python
forms, positive_forms = 500, 180
duplicate_forms, positive_duplicates = 20, 12
unique_responses = forms - duplicate_forms
positive_enterprises = positive_forms - positive_duplicates
selected_enterprises = 600
unknown = selected_enterprises - unique_responses
print(round(100 * positive_forms / forms, 1))
print(round(100 * positive_enterprises / unique_responses, 1))
print(round(100 * unique_responses / selected_enterprises, 1))
print([round(100 * positive_enterprises / selected_enterprises, 1),
       round(100 * (positive_enterprises + unknown) / selected_enterprises, 1)])
```

```text
36.0
35.0
80.0
[28.0, 48.0]
```

The raw form proportion is 36%. After verification, 168 of 480 enterprises have positive intentions: 35%. Response is 80%, with 120 missing outcomes. The 28%–48% bounds apply only to the 600 selected enterprises; they have no confidence level and do not address informal-business coverage. A defensible statement is: “Among 480 enterprises with valid, unique responses, 168 (35%) stated an intention to hire during the defined horizon.” Report selection, missing responses, frame coverage, and collection dates alongside it. Do not describe 168 as vacancies or completed hires. Reaching informal businesses requires a separate coverage strategy and checking for overlap.
